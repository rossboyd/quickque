# Code of Conduct

Quickque is a small open-source project. Participants are expected to make
technical discussion useful, direct, and safe for people with different
backgrounds and levels of experience.

## Expected behavior

* Be respectful and assume good faith while challenging ideas.
* Explain disagreement with evidence, concrete examples, or a proposed
  alternative.
* Keep issues and reviews about the code, documentation, design, or user
  problem rather than the person.
* Give credit for existing work and preserve third-party attribution.
* Protect privacy: use synthetic examples and remove secrets, private scripts,
  meeting content, audio, transcripts, and personal data.
* Accept that a browser check is not Mac verification and state what you
  actually tested.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment, threats, discrimination, personal attacks, doxxing, unwanted
contact, sexualized attention, deliberate disruption, impersonation, and
publishing someone else's private information are not welcome. Neither are
malicious submissions, credential exposure, or pressure to bypass the
project's local-first privacy boundaries.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, close, or remove contributions and may limit
participation when behavior violates this policy or makes collaboration
unsafe. They will consider the context, pattern, intent, and impact of the
behavior. A good-faith technical disagreement is not, by itself, a violation.

Do not put sensitive conduct details in a public issue. This project does not
publish a guaranteed private moderation channel; if GitHub offers a private
reporting option for this repository, use it, and otherwise share only a
non-sensitive summary publicly while waiting for a safe follow-up path.

This policy applies to repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions,
commits, and project-related spaces where a participant is representing
Quickque.
